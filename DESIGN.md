# Monfly v2 — design and interaction guide

The taste that makes Monfly feel the way it does, written down so it survives.
Read it before building UI; when a decision here changes, change it here first.
Tokens live in `src/app.css`; the architecture is in `README.md`.

## Principles

1. **Calm surfaces, crisp outlines.** White cards on a grey canvas, separated by
   the gap — never by borders. Controls wear the near-black 1px hairline.
2. **Colour gives it life.** Monfly is not a muted design. The accents carry
   meaning — an icon wears its accent, a state its tone — so the calm surfaces
   never read as grey. Neutral is for text and structure, not for everything.
3. **Grids of cards that fill their cells.** Equal heights, aligned edges, one
   homogeneous flow. Never a content-sized box centred in empty space.
4. **Motion is feedback, not decoration.** Every animation answers something:
   you pressed this, this appeared, this moved from there to here. Fast in,
   soft out.
5. **Real data paints first.** Figures are server-rendered; animation only
   enhances what is already on screen. Nothing animates before mount.
6. **Reuse the vocabulary.** New UI is composed from `$lib/components/ui`, not
   restyled per page. If a primitive can't do it, extend the primitive.
7. **Accessible by default.** Keyboard, visible focus, reduced motion, labels —
   a hover-only affordance also shows on focus and on touch.

## Tokens

| Group    | Tokens                                                   | Use                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brand    | `blue` `violet` `lime` `ink`                             | Accents; constant in light and dark                                                                                                                              |
| Surfaces | `window` (top bar) `canvas` (page) `card` `sunken`       | `sunken` for inset chips, inactive tabs, highlights                                                                                                              |
| Lines    | `line` `line-strong` `hairline`                          | `line` divides and frames layers; `hairline` outlines controls                                                                                                   |
| Text     | `fg` `fg-muted` `fg-subtle`                              | Body, labels, placeholders and quiet figures                                                                                                                     |
| State    | `positive` `negative` `spent`                            | On track / over budget, errors, leaving (log out); `spent` is the pastel red every expense figure wears — money out is not a fault, so it never takes `negative` |
| Radii    | `--radius-card` 1.5rem · `--radius-chip` 1rem            | Cards · pills, popovers, menus                                                                                                                                   |
| Easing   | `--ease-out-quint` (almost everything) · `--ease-spring` | `ease-spring` overshoots — small gestures only                                                                                                                   |

Only use tokens that exist. A class like `bg-elevated` compiles to nothing and
fails silently — the old tooltip rendered transparent that way.

**Tints.** An accent at low opacity makes a chip: `bg-blue/12 text-blue`,
`bg-violet/12 text-violet`, `bg-negative/12 text-negative`. Lime is too light
to be a glyph on white, so it inverts: `bg-lime/30` with the glyph mixed toward
ink. On dark surfaces it's light enough to be the glyph again:
`dark:bg-lime/15 dark:text-lime`. A line, a dot or a fill in a palette
colour takes it at that glyph's weight through `.ink-tint`: set `--tint`, paint
with `--ink-tint` (the colour itself on dark). A pill that narrows something (the ledger's _Show_) wears its tint the same way and keeps the hairline's shape: its rim is the colour a step darker.

**The palette** (`$lib/components/ui/palette.ts`) is every colour a person can
give something: the brand blue, violet and lime, then nine pastels — sky, teal,
mint, lemon, peach, coral, rose, pink, lavender. The pastels are OKLCH tokens
defined per theme in app.css: soft on white cards, a touch deeper and richer on
dark so they glow instead of washing out. User-chosen colours come from here
and nowhere else; add a colour by adding a token pair and a palette entry.
Choices are stored by palette id (`User.colors`), never as a CSS value, so
retuning a colour updates every place that wears it.

## Type

- **Space Grotesk (`font-display`)** for headings and figures: light weights,
  `tracking-tight`, `tabular` digits so numbers never reflow mid-tween.
  Page titles `text-6xl`/`7xl` light; card titles `text-2xl` medium; figures
  through `Figure` (`sm` → `xl`).
- **Outfit (`font-sans`)** for everything else. Labels `text-[0.9375rem]
text-fg-muted`; meta `text-sm` / `text-xs`.
- Money: always to the cent, in the currency's home locale (`formatMoney`) —
  never rounded to whole units. Only chart labels go compact ("$24.5k"), and
  their tooltip gives the exact figure. Missing values show `—`. UI copy is
  English, sentence case.
- Figures that can grow never spill or truncate. Where there's room to take,
  their box grows instead: the category chips widen leftward, over the dial's
  free space, and keep `text-xl`. Where there isn't, the figure fits its box:
  the box is a container (`@container`) and the figure `.fit-figure`, with
  `--fit` the size it wants and `--chars` the figure it's sized for — the
  longest of a row, so neighbours match (an account's two figures). It keeps
  its size until the room runs out, then shrinks just enough.
- Names wrap rather than truncate or shrink wherever a card has height to give
  (an account block's name): `text-balance` so the lines come out even,
  `wrap-anywhere` so one long word breaks instead of widening the box, and the
  control beside them keeps its words on one line (`whitespace-nowrap`) and
  drops under them (`flex-wrap` over a `basis`) rather than squeezing either.
  Only a column that can't grow truncates — the ledger's — and it tells the rest
  in a tip.

## Layout

- Page gutters `px-4 sm:px-6 lg:px-8`; cards `gap-4`; the header is `h-20` and
  sticky.
- Things side by side stay level: give header rows a fixed minimum height
  (`MeterStat` uses `min-h-9`, the action button's height) so one row's button
  doesn't push its neighbour's bar down.
- When a card holds less than its cell, keep the card full-size and anchor the
  content inside it: header row on top (title left, action right), content at
  the bottom. `/dashboard` and `/signup` are the references.
- **One fact per column, in the order a row is read.** A table column holds one
  thing — the category, the amount, the account, the description, the day —
  never two run together with a separator. Eyes cross a row hardest at its left
  and less at every step across (the F-pattern), so the ledger and the two
  card-less lists lead with what a row is — the category's glyph, then its
  name — and put what it cost right beside it, where the answer is never a
  row's width from its question. The account and the description follow; the
  day closes the row, where a mail list keeps its timestamps, with the row's
  actions after it. Figures are right-aligned so their digits stack; words
  and days are left-aligned, a day so its whole date unrolls out of its start. Columns are 24 px apart (`px-3` on every cell; the lists carry it as `gap-x-6`) and the outer ones line up with the card's content, while a row's highlight overhangs them by 12 px into the card's padding, rounded (`rounded-lg`): the scroller pulls out by that much (`-mx-3`), so an overlay scrollbar rides there rather than over the last column.
- **Width goes to the words.** The ledger's columns name widths in rem, not
  shares, each holding its widest value — the day its whole date, "September
  30, 2026 · 12:59 AM" — and the description, the one column that grows, takes
  whatever is left (with it hidden, the last column before the actions does).
  A wider screen widens the note instead of pulling a row's facts apart. Every
  drawn column at its width is the floor, 57rem with all of them; under it the
  ledger scrolls sideways rather than crushing a description against an orb,
  and the category's glyph and name stay pinned at the left, so a row never
  loses its name. The card-less lists size their tracks to their content the
  same way, the description taking the rest.
- **Every table names its columns** in a header that sticks to the top of its
  scroller and orders by them (`SortHeader`), draws `—` for a value a row
  hasn't got, and rules a line under each row but the last — the card's own
  edge closes the list, and a second line beside it reads as an empty row. The
  lines are the only part that is a choice: `dividers` is on by default and a
  quieter list turns it off. Rows sit 2 px apart, so two picked ones read as
  two blocks of colour rather than one long one, and a heading never takes a
  control of its own: what acts on the whole list is a `PillButton` saying so
  in words, at the right of the card where the description ends.
- **A list's columns belong to the list, not to its rows.** Where the rows
  aren't a `<table>`, the list is the grid and each row takes its tracks with
  `grid-cols-subgrid` (`UnassignedCard`). A grid per row would size each one to
  its own content, and a short day beside a long one would walk the column
  along with it.
- **A long list scrolls inside its card**, rather than stretching the column
  past the view: the ledger caps at `max-h-[32rem]`, the two card-less lists on
  `/transactions` at `22rem`, and the dashboard's Transactions tab at whatever
  height Tips gives its card. What the list is answered by — its title, count,
  figure and the box that picks the lot — stays above the scroller, in reach
  wherever the rows are.

Dashboard compositions live in `$lib/components/dashboard` (`MeterStat`,
`SpentThisMonth`, `BudgetEditor`, `ExpensesCard`, …); the shell in
`$lib/components/layout`.

**The expenses dial** is a pie of the whole period: the top four categories
are wedges of the total, clockwise from 270°, and the hatch is everything
else — the long tail. Each wedge wears its category's colour, and so does its
chip: the person picks it from the chip's orb, and it's stored in
`User.colors` (see `$lib/colors`). Until a category has a choice, it takes the
rank colours — blue, violet, lime, sky — skipping any colour a choice already
holds, so two of the four only match when someone chose that. A recolour
eases the wedge and the orb together. Every mark reads one angle scale, the
way a grammar-of-graphics polar chart does (TanStack Charts' `pie` and its
`startAngle`). As in the mockup, a `--hairline` divides each part from the
next — wedge from wedge, the last wedge from the hatch — running straight
from the hub, a small fixed four-point star, out past the rim to a black tag
whose tip points back at the hub. The wedges stay soft, blurred into one
another. Small parts' dividers ease apart to at least 14°, standing a few
degrees off their edges rather than bending (the soft colour hides the
degrees; nothing hides a kink), and it all turns and morphs with the wedges.
The chips may cover part of the dial: their own blur keeps it looking right.
Their row is at least 62% of the card and widens leftward when its longest
figure or label needs it; its two columns stay equal, the widest chip setting
both.

**The accounts column** opens with an overview card at its own height — the
"Accounts" header and actions, every active account's total, this month's net
movement as a tinted chip — a dot, where a pointer can hover, until pointed
at or focused — and a share bar where each account's slice wears
its colour. What v1's total holds beyond the accounts — money that moved with
no account, a total typed in by hand — takes one more slice, Unknown: no
palette colour but the hatch inside a hairline, a ring in the legend, sized by
how far it swings either way. Its tooltip splits it into money in, money out
and adjustments, each signed the way it moves the total. Pressing a slice, or
its name in the legend — a slice can be a sliver too thin to hit — leaves that
account out of the total and the change until it's pressed again, and the label
says how many are left out. What's left out is per browser, in the
`accounts-left-out` cookie, which the dashboard's server load reads so SSR
draws the total without it after a reload or a trip to another page. Below it two featured accounts, a card each, split the rest of the
column 50/50 (`flex-1` on a zero basis) — each block's header sits on top and
its figures anchor to the bottom, so spare height spreads instead of pooling
under the second card. An account has one colour everywhere: orb, sparkle,
slice. They're the accounts marked main and secondary (the
chevrons, a picker's glyph, open two `Select`s under a blue chip with the credit
card accounts are drawn with), else the oldest, in the order they were added;
a slot filled that way says _By default_ under its name in the picker.
Each header's filter shows the account now or as an earlier month of this year
ended. Each block keeps the mockup — balance and this month's
spending over orbiting rings that turn opposite ways — and its balance orb is
the account's colour, worn by its sparkle too: main starts lime, secondary
blue. The account's name and "To review" are links with an arrow that slides
in: the name to the account itself, open on the accounts page
(`accountPageHref`), and "To review" to its rows in the ledger (`?account=`). A long name wraps,
balanced, and the header's filter drops under it once there's no room beside
it (see Type); the header's
open-accounts button is an `IconButton` with `href`.

**The income chart** keeps the mockup's hatched columns and accent caps on
real data: this quarter by month (the default), this month by week, this year
by quarter or by month, all time by year. Bars scale to the tallest one; a past bucket
with nothing in it keeps a hairline, and one still to come is a dashed empty
slot with no figure. Hovering or focusing a bar shows its exact income, entry
count and share. The total keeps its lime symbol while its number counts.

The gear left of the period (`IncomeSettings`, a small `IconButton` level
with the `Select`) opens the chart's settings under a violet chip — violet is
configuration: **This year by** quarters or months (`Segmented`), then
**Amounts over bars** and **Still to come** (`Switch`es). Changes apply at
once. Past eight bars, the figures shrink and drop their decimal ("$25k");
turning off what's still to come folds those slots away. The settings are
per browser, in the `income-view` cookie, and so is the period picked beside
the gear: the dashboard's server load reads it so SSR draws the chosen period
and view after a reload or a trip to another page, and the shared database
never sees it.

**The Transactions tab**, beside Tips and Loans under Income, is the ledger at
a glance: the last five transactions, newest first, in the ledger's order —
the category's glyph and name, what it cost, the account's orb (the colour its
block wears, its name in a tip, a dashed ring for none), the day and its time —
and nothing to act on. So its rows don't highlight and its header names the
columns in words, without `SortHeader`: five rows have no order to pick. The
date stays short, its time under it a size down (`DateLabel` with
`unroll={false}` and `time`): a third of the grid can't keep room for the whole
one, and the time on the same line cut the category short. A label sits on top, with _See all_ closing its line on the
way to the ledger — its arrow always shown, since it's the card's one way on,
and pushing under the pointer — and the list anchors to the foot. The card's
height is the Tips card's (`CardTabs`), never the rows' — stacked, under `xl`,
the slot keeps a floor of its own (`min-h-96`), room for the tip's globe and
most of the rows: past it the rows scroll under the column names, which stick,
and the foot fades while more wait below. It reads
the ledger's own cache entry the first time the tab opens, not with the page;
with nothing recorded it says so in the Loans tab's shape. While that first
read is on its way, a `Loader` stands in the middle of the card and fades as
the rows deal in under it.

**The shortcuts page** (`/shortcuts`, behind the header's plus) lists every
place that can be a tab, a card each in the dashboard's grid: its glyph in a
chip of its colour, what the page is for, whether it's in the header, and the
hotkey that goes there. A `Switch` pins or unpins it and the header follows at
once; a refusal puts the switch back and says so on the card. The heading
counts what's in the header. Overview wears a lock where the others have a
switch: it's home. Pressed four times in a row, reddening a little more with each
press, it springs open and asks in a `ConfirmDialog` whether to take it out;
taken out, it gets a switch like the rest, and pinning it again locks it.
Under the cards, **Activity** reads the last eight weeks of changes in three
cards on the same grid: changes by week (the income chart's columns, each week
under its Monday but the running one under _Now_), the
shortcuts changed most, and where changes are made — a switch, a tab's ✕, the
lock. Each card is one series, so one colour, violet, configuration's; the
most-changed bars wear each shortcut's own colour, beside its glyph and name.
Every count is written out, and tooltips add the pinned and taken-out split.

**The transactions page** starts with what never got an account: one card
listing every card-less transaction in two groups. _Counted in your total_
are those dated since the first account — the dashboard's Unknown slice, with
its figure beside the heading — and giving one an account moves it into that
account's balance. _Before your first account_ are already in the balance it
was opened with, so an account only records where they came from. Checkboxes
pick rows (shift-click takes a range; a group's box takes the group, with a
dash for some), picked rows tint blue, and while any are picked a bar sticks
to the bottom of the view: how many, what would move where, the account
(`Select`, each with its colour dot, main first) and the action. Each card has the ledger's bar (`LedgerToolbar`): its own search, kind and columns, and _Pick all_ picks only what they leave. What an
assignment did is said in a positive `Badge` under the first card's header for
5 s; another before it goes updates that same badge and starts the 5 s over.

_Transfer_, beside _New_ in the ledger's header, opens the panel on a new
transfer: the plus's lime becomes lavender — the colour a transfer's rows wear,
their category's — and its chip the send glyph, _New transfer_. The fields are
the amount, `TransferAccounts` (the first account towards the next one), the day
and time and the note: no direction and no category, a transfer being both ways and
filed under _Transfer_. The pill is there once there are two accounts, making
room as it arrives, and is held down in lavender while the panel writes one;
pressing _New_ over a half-written transfer turns it into a transaction and
keeps what was typed, and the other way round. Both sides land in the ledger as
rows of their own, each on its account, their amounts in the lavender at a
glyph's weight rather than green or red: the money was neither earned nor
spent. Reading either side, the panel says _Moved_ with no sign, and _From_ and
_To_ with their orbs where a row says _Account_; its pencil rewrites the
transfer whole, and _Delete_ asks about both sides. _Activity_ and _Where it
went_ leave transfers out.

The ledger opens on the whole record. A `Select` in its header narrows it,
and _Where it went_, to one month (_All time_, then every month from this one
back to the first on record); while a month is picked, arrows beside it walk
to the next and previous one, gone at either end. _Activity_ keeps reading the
whole record: it draws the picked month with the two before and the one after,
and sets the other three back (faded and greyed, their figures and tips still there to read). _Where it went_ paints each bar in its category's own colour (`categoryColor`, the one its glyph wears in the ledger), so a category keeps its colour whatever rank it takes from one month to the next.

**The accounts page** (`/cards`, the Accounts shortcut) has the transactions
page's shape: the title beside four figures (what the accounts hold, what came
in and went out this month, how many there are), then the accounts and their
chart on the left and a 23rem panel beside them (`$lib/components/accounts`).

- **Each account is drawn as its card** (`AccountTile`): its brand's mark,
  issuer and place along the top, a chip, `•••• last4`, and its name and balance at the foot, over a
  blurred glow in its colour and a sparkline of its balance across the chart's
  range. The place is the dashboard's (`accountPlace`), so the two never
  disagree: the role it holds, in a chip of its colour — or, where it fills a
  slot nobody chose, that slot _by default_ on a dashed rim instead of the
  fill. The panel's _Featured as_ and the editor say the same. The whole card
  is one button that opens it in the panel, and the address follows the pick
  (`?account=`, replaced as the ledger's filters are) so a link or a reload
  opens that account; the mark heading its label is the bank or
  network its name or issuer names (`$lib/account-icons`, 0020), on a white
  plate in its own colours, nothing where no brand is named — pressed, it opens
  (as _Icon_ in its `⋯` does, the same `AccountIconOptions`)
  the brands (each on its plate, the one read from the name checked in
  `fg-subtle`, a pick in blue) and _Automatic_ to go back to the name; its `⋯` sits
  in the corner. The open one lifts and wears a rim in its colour. After the
  cards comes the next one's slot: dashed and hatched like the income chart's
  slots still to come, held down in lime while the panel writes a new account.
- **The panel** is the ledger panel's three modes in the same colours — the
  eye reads, the pencil edits, the plus adds — and eases its height between
  them. Reading, it's the account's orb (press it to recolour), its dial, this
  month's in, out and net, and its facts; under it, _Where it went_ reads the
  account's rows over the chart's range. The fields are v1's (name, kind,
  issuer, last four, balance) plus v2's featured place and, for a new account,
  its colour. A balance runs either way — _Holds_ or _Owes_ — and a new one on
  an existing account says, before saving, the correction it records. A fourth
  mode moves money from the account on show: the send toggle before the pencil
  (held down in lavender, as the pencil is in violet), or _Move money_ in its
  `⋯`, opens the ledger panel's transfer fields with it as _From_, under a
  lavender chip, _Moving money_. Its _In_, _Out_ and _Net this month_ are what
  really arrived and left, transfers included, so _Net_ is the dial's change;
  under them a line says how much of it was theirs moving, each part in
  lavender — "Includes $500.00 moved in from and $2,000.00 out to your other
  accounts" ([0022](docs/decisions/0022-account-figures-count-transfers.md)).
- **The dial** (`BalanceGauge`) is a credit score's, read as money: 240° open
  at the foot, from the lowest balance over the range at its left to the
  highest at its right. The account's colour fills it to today's balance,
  where a white knob rides; rounded segments mark the room above it. The
  balance sits inside, with this month's change as a tinted chip under it.
- **The chart** (`BalanceChart`) is a line per account in its colour, taken to
  a glyph's weight on white, over round-step gridlines; the open account's line
  is heavier over a soft fill of its colour. Each line ends in a tag of today's
  balance in its colour, nudged apart where two would touch, with a leader back
  to the line. Pointing at the chart — or arrowing along it, a slider over the
  days — draws a dashed guide, a ringed dot on each line and a card of every
  balance that day; a press picks the nearest line. The legend toggles lines
  (the last one stays). Under the days a track holds pins — an account added,
  a balance set by hand — each the account's orb in its turning `OrbitRing` on
  a stem, neighbours turning opposite ways; its tip says which it was. The range sits last on an axis of its own: a soft
  blue column between two ink marks rises behind the one chosen.
- **The menu** on a card: view, edit, _Move money_ (lavender, the send glyph;
  there only with another account to move it to), _Feature as_ (blue, a submenu with a
  check; a slot held by default wears its check in `fg-subtle` beside _By
  default_, _Not featured_ is disabled since only another account takes the
  slot, and a note under the options says so), _Icon_ (a pastel of the
  account's own colour; the brands its mark opens, and the only way to give one
  to an account whose name names none), its transactions (lavender, the ledger's Account colour), then under
  rules _Archive_ (peach) and _Delete_ (`negative`, with a `ConfirmDialog`).
  Archived accounts gather in a card under the chart, each with _Restore_ and
  a delete of its own.

**The insights page** (`/insights`, `$lib/components/insights`) is a space to
turn the numbers over without changing them — and to take them to an
accountant. Everything on it reads the ledger's own record and is worked out in
the browser ([0023](docs/decisions/0023-insights-read-the-ledger-in-the-browser.md));
what it's set to lasts the visit and is written nowhere.

- **The hero** is the title and a line saying nothing here changes a record,
  with two pickers at its right: _What counts_ (the ledger filters' pill and
  popover, in their blue: every account and category ticked, untick one to leave
  it out of every chart, _Count everything_ to put it all back) and the period
  (`Select`: _Last 12 months_, then every year from this one back to the first
  on record; this year to begin with). Under them, `Highlights`: pills on the
  canvas, each a glyph in a round chip of its colour, a muted label and the fact
  — best month, where most went, the share kept, the largest expense, no-spend
  days, the longest run of them. While something is left out a line says what,
  with _Count everything_ in violet.
- **Every change is measured against the stretch before** — last year, or the
  twelve months before — and only as far as this one has come: a year still
  running meets _this time in 2025_. With nothing on record there, a figure says
  _Nothing earlier to compare_ and a chart draws no comparison.
- **Money over time** (`TrendCard`, the reference's performance card) is one
  figure over the stretch: spent in rose, received in mint, kept in blue, the
  share kept in violet. _Weeks_ draws a column of dots a week (`DotColumns`):
  filled from the baseline as far as the week reaches against the fullest, the
  rest ghosts in `line`, and a figure below zero hanging under the baseline in
  rose; weeks still to come step back. _Months_ draws a stepped bar a month
  (`StepBars`): an edge in the colour over a wash that fades to the baseline,
  flush against the next, the stretch before dashed across their middles. Both
  read like the balance chart — a guide, a ringed dot, a card, arrow keys along
  a slider — and the dots name the week in an ink tag under the axis. Under the
  chart, the four figures are also its picker: a radio group whose raised ground
  (an ink edge over a sunken wash) slides to the chosen one, its chip turned to
  ink. A share moves in points (`+2.1 pts`), money in percent.
- **Beside it**, three `SparkStat` cards: entries, the average expense and the
  average income, each a figure that fits its box (`.fit-figure`), its change —
  green or rose where one way is good news — and a curve month by month over a
  comb of hairlines, pinned and named at its peak.
- **Categories over time** is a stepped line per biggest category, in its own
  colour over a wash of it, _Each month_ or as a _Running total_ (`Segmented`).
  Its legend is a `Checkbox` a line (the last one stays) and pointing at a name
  sets the other lines back; the reading lists every line that month with its
  change from the month before, rose up and green down.
- **Where income went** (`IncomeArc`) is a half dial of what came in: the three
  biggest categories in their colours, the rest hatched as the dashboard dial's
  tail, what was kept in lime; spending past income fills it with spending and
  says by how much. Its parts and legend light together under the pointer.
- **Spending pace** (`PaceCard`) sets the day-by-day figures — per day, no-spend
  days, the biggest day, the busiest weekday — beside everything spent so far,
  day by day, in violet over the stretch before in `line-strong`. The day being
  read wears a soft violet column.
- **Every day, dotted** (`SpendingCalendar`) is a dot a day, a column a week and
  a row a weekday, sized and deepened by the quarter the day's spending falls in;
  the biggest day rings. Beside it the categories that took the most, each a
  press that redraws the dots as that category's days alone, in its colour.
  Narrower than 40rem, the calendar scrolls sideways in the card.
- **What if** (`WhatIf`) is the sandbox, under a violet chip — configuration —
  saying nothing is saved: a `Slider` from −50% to +50% for everything received,
  and one from −100% to +50% for each of the four biggest categories, each from
  _As it was_. Beside them, what would have stayed and the share kept, each
  against what happened, and month by month the what-if in violet over the
  ghost of what happened (rose below zero). _As it was_ puts every slider back.
  Only this card takes the what-if.
- **Accounts in and out** (`AccountFlows`) splits each account's bar in two, in
  mint and rose, against the busiest, the room left hatched; what stayed closes
  the row, and the exact figures are in a tip.
- **Statement** is a `CardTabs` card — _By month_ and _By category_ — of tables
  to hand over, every figure to the cent, a total under each, their column names
  sticking as they scroll, and _Download CSV_: the lot as plain decimals under a
  line naming the currency, for a spreadsheet to add up.

Rules:

- **Exits are quicker than entrances** — about three quarters of the time.
- **Exits start from the current state**, so interrupting an entrance never jumps.
- **Change state by moving or morphing, never by blinking** between two icons.
- **Small things may be playful.** A glyph's gesture can overshoot
  (`--ease-spring`); anything that moves layout or a large surface stays on
  ease-out-quint.
- **Animate compositor properties** (opacity, scale, translate, rotate). Width
  only where it buys spatial continuity: the meter fill, the back button's room,
  the change chip opening out of its dot.
- **Reduced motion:** `app.css` flattens CSS transitions; Motion and GSAP code
  checks `prefersReducedMotion()` and falls back to opacity-only or instant;
  blobatar goes still on its own.
- **SSR renders the final state.** Anything the server can't know — the stored
  theme — waits invisible and fades in once placed, rather than flashing.
- In the Chrome automation window animations crawl (hidden page, throttled
  frames). Judge end states there, not feel.

## Icons

Every glyph is Lucide's drawing under Lucide's name, at stroke `1.75`, `size-4`
in menus and pills. Static glyphs come from `@lucide/svelte`. Where a glyph
should move on its own, three Svelte 5 sets are installed beside it
([0010](docs/decisions/0010-animated-icons.md)); React-only sets don't run here.

| Set                   | Package and import                                       | Glyphs                                    | Plays                                                                                                                       | Reduced motion                                        |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Lucide                | `@lucide/svelte/icons/eye`                               | Lucide, all                               | Our own gestures, in CSS, on highlight                                                                                      | Follows `app.css`                                     |
| Moving Icons          | `@jis3r/icons/icons/eye`                                 | Lucide, about 550, motion drawn per glyph | Its `animate` prop, and its own hover                                                                                       | Ignored: gate `animate` with `prefersReducedMotion()` |
| Animated Color Icons  | `@animated-color-icons/lucide-svelte/Eye.svelte`         | Lucide, all                               | CSS under its own `:hover`, an `.al-icon-wrapper:hover` ancestor, and from `app.css` a highlighted row or a focused wrapper | Follows `app.css`                                     |
| Svelte Animated Icons | `svelte-animated-icons` (`EyeHero`, `…Ion`, `…Flowbite`) | Heroicons, Ionicons, Flowbite             | Draw transition on hover or click (`event`)                                                                                 | Follows `app.css`                                     |

- **Pick by glyph.** Lucide when it stays still. To move: Moving Icons when it
  has the glyph, else Animated Color Icons, which has all of Lucide.
  `svelte-animated-icons` only where no Lucide glyph fits, and never among
  Lucide glyphs — a different drawing in a group reads as a mistake.
- **A gesture must not erase its glyph.** A few Moving Icons write themselves
  in from nothing — `plus` and `x` start at no opacity with their strokes
  offset by their whole length. That is a fine entrance and a poor hover: the
  icon goes missing exactly as the pointer reaches it, and crossing the
  control's edge restarts it, so it sits half-drawn — a plus reading as a minus.
  Those play once as they appear (`trigger="mount"`) or on a state they report
  (`play`, as every check does), never on `control`. Glyphs that move rather
  than redraw — the back arrow's head, the pencil, the eye — keep the pointer.
- **A family of glyphs takes one set, unless the gesture is the point.** Animated
  Color Icons gives the same staggered `scale-pop` to 27 of the 37 glyphs we draw
  from it, so a row of its chips all gesture alike — which reads as one animation
  repeated, not as each glyph having its own. Where that sameness is what a
  person notices, a glyph takes Moving Icons instead, which draws its motion per
  glyph: the transfer's arrows shuttle, the tag leans, the gauge's needle sweeps,
  hand-coins throws its coin, calendar-days pulses its dots. Each kind carries
  both its drawings and the set that plays the moving one in `$lib/categories`
  (`Glyph`, from `AnimatedIcon`) — the still one for a table, the moving one for
  the picker — so a table can mix sets per entry. The other twelve category kinds
  and `TrendCard`'s _Spent_ and _Kept_ stay on Animated Color Icons because
  Moving Icons has no drawing for them; leave a glyph there rather than reach for
  a third set.
- **Name it Lucide's name, prefixed by its set** when a file mixes sets:
  `MovingEye`, `ColorTrendingUp`.
- **Place it with `AnimatedIcon`** (`$lib/components/ui`): `<AnimatedIcon icon={MovingSettings} set="moving" />`. It finds the nearest control around it — a button, a link, a menu row, anything focusable, or a surface that asks for it with `data-icon-host` (insights' highlight pills, which read rather than act) — and plays while that's hovered or focused, never under the pointer on the glyph alone (it's `pointer-events-none`, which also keeps Animated Color Icons' `<title>` from opening a native tooltip). `trigger="mount"` plays it once as it appears — the chip heading a popover, a tooltip's own heading; `trigger="visible"` once as it scrolls into view, for a card's emblem down a page you scroll, where `mount` would spend the gesture below the fold; `play` holds it from outside — a check as its option is chosen, a gear while its popover is open. It gates Moving Icons under reduced motion and sets `.al-play` for Animated Color Icons, whose effects in use are in `app.css`; add the next when an icon brings it.
- **Decorative, so hidden.** The label beside it says what it is; both
  Lucide-based sets label themselves `role="img"`, so wrap them `aria-hidden`.
- **Where:** Moving Icons for the dashboard's pencils, gear, chevrons-up-down, hand-coins and review arrow, the header's back arrow, new tab and tab close, the user menu's gear, bell and log-out, the tips' arrows, the ledger tools' arrow-left-right, tag, calendar-days, eye and rotate-ccw, a row's own eye, pencil, copy and trash-2, the panel's eye, pencil, plus and send-horizontal (a transfer, with the _Transfer_ pill, the accounts panel's toggle and _Move money_), and every check and dash (`Checkbox`, `Select`, the ledger tools), and insights' own change arrows — arrow-up-right and arrow-down-right beside a `SparkStat`'s percentage, circle-arrow-up and circle-arrow-down beside a `PaceCard` figure, the transfer and tag a category wears, and `TrendCard`'s gauge and hand-coins with `PaceCard`'s calendar-days; Animated Color Icons for the target, external link, profile, list filter, columns, trends (the ledger's type filter and the panel's _Which way_, where each option wears its own in a `size-6` chip tinted like the figures it stands for — rose out, green in), text, credit card (the featured accounts' chip too) and banknote, and every category's own glyph in the picker. The accounts total's change arrow stays Lucide: Motion already leans it, as `TransferAccounts`' arrow-down-up is, which GSAP turns over.

## Floating layers

- **Always bits-ui** — `Tooltip`, `Popover`, `DropdownMenu`. It positions with
  Floating UI and handles focus, Escape and ARIA. `use:anchor` (raw Floating UI)
  is only for bespoke overlays bits-ui can't express.
- **Presence pattern:** `forceMount` on the content, then the `child` snippet
  with `{#if open}` around `in:pop out:pop`, and
  `origin-(--bits-floating-transform-origin)` so it grows from its anchor.
- **Chain, never replace, a trigger's handlers.** bits-ui hands its trigger's
  element `onpointerenter`, `onpointerleave`, `onfocus`, `onblur`, `onclick`
  and more through the spread `props`. Writing your own `onpointerleave={…}`
  after the spread replaces bits-ui's, and the layer never hears the pointer
  leave: it sticks open and stops responding. Call theirs, then yours — see
  `chain` in `ShareBarPicker` or the `onclick` in `ThemeToggle`.
- **Surface:** `rounded-[var(--radius-chip)] border border-line bg-card shadow-lg`.
  Tooltips are `rounded-lg px-2.5 py-1.5 text-xs`, and their arrow is part of
  the surface: filled with the card, its two edges in the border's line,
  tucked 1px under the border so the two join.
- **A layer of controls is as wide as its rows need.** Menus and the ledger's
  filters start at their default width and grow with what they hold, up to the
  screen less its gutters — `w-max` over `min-w-64` for menus (`min-w-60` for a
  row's actions), over 22rem for the filters, capped at
  `max-w-[calc(100vw-2rem)]` — so a long name or a field is never cut short or
  squeezed out of its box. Its height stops short of the room Floating UI says
  is left (`--bits-floating-available-height`), and only then does it scroll. A
  popover of prose keeps its measure (`w-[min(20rem,calc(100vw-2rem))]`):
  unwrapped, its sentences would read worse than the width would help.
- **Placement:** open on the side that doesn't cover what the layer explains,
  and anchor to the exact point it describes (the meter's tip points at the fill's end, and opens below the bar; a column's detail points at its bar's top, so an empty bar's opens by the baseline).
- **Menus:** items `h-10 rounded-[0.625rem] px-1.5`, highlight `bg-sunken`.
  Each icon sits in a `size-7 rounded-lg` chip tinted with its accent (see
  Tints), glyph `size-4` at stroke 1.75, and makes its gesture on highlight.
  Accents follow meaning: blue for you (profile), violet for configuration
  (settings), lime for what's new (notifications), `negative` for leaving (log
  out). Separators `mx-1 my-1.5 h-px bg-line`; shortcuts right-aligned in `Kbd`;
  disabled items grey their chip and say why ("Soon").
- **Confirming** something that can't be taken back is `ConfirmDialog`
  (bits-ui `AlertDialog`): the floating surface centred over a dimmed page, the
  action named on its own button — "Delete", never "OK" — and what the question
  is about repeated in a sunken block beneath it. It stays open while the action
  runs and says what went wrong in place, so an answer never lands behind a
  layer that has already closed.
- **Row actions** sit at the end of the row, in a column of their own: one `⋯`
  `IconButton`, in the same place down every row, opening the menu above. Their
  order is the order of the day — look, change, complete, take away, and last,
  under a rule, the one that can't be undone, in `negative`. Each chip wears the
  colour of what it touches, the column's own pastel where it has one.
- **A name cut short tells the rest in a tip.** The ledger's account column
  keeps its width and truncates; under the pointer or focus the account opens a
  rich `Tooltip` above it: its whole name beside its orb, the card in v1's own
  terms on one line — role, issuer, `•••• last4`, kind, only what was filled
  in — and where it stands in the dashboard's words: bank balance, tracked this
  month, net this month (green in, the expense red out). An account no longer
  active says so instead of showing figures it no longer has. On a transfer's
  side the tip tells the whole move instead, since the other side can sit rows
  or pages away: _Moved between your accounts_, then the account it left with
  its orb, card line and _From_, a dashed line down from that orb to the next
  with the amount beside it in lavender, and the account it landed in, _To_ — a
  dashed ring and _No longer active_ for an end that's gone.
- **Popovers** open with the same chip beside a title and a one-line note, its
  accent saying what the layer does: violet configures (Income chart), blue
  picks accounts (Featured accounts), lime sets the budget — the colour of the
  meter it moves, inverted as lime always is on white.

## The header

Brand → back → tabs → theme → account.

- **Back** (dashed `IconButton`) shows on every (app) route but `/dashboard`.
  It walks the app's own history and goes home when there is none, so a deep
  link never sends anyone out of Monfly.
- **Tabs:** one shared surface carries the browser-tab shape between tabs; tab
  widths stay fixed (the ✕ is always rendered) so nothing reflows mid-slide.
  Scrolled, the page no longer meets the tab, so the surface lets go: it lifts
  off into a flat box behind the tab, rounded like the tabs
  (`--radius-chip`), and a small ink drop drips out
  beneath it, marking the tab you're on. Back at the top it runs in reverse.
- **Shortcuts:** the tabs are the person's own. Overview comes first, locked —
  it's home — then whatever they pinned on `/shortcuts`, in that page's order. The dashed plus opens the page; a tab's ✕ takes its
  shortcut away, and closing the tab you're on moves you to the one before it.
  Each tab wears its shortcut's glyph in its colour, drawn from the same place as its card's (`ShortcutIcon`), so the two never drift apart, and plays it under the pointer. They're stored on the account, so every device shows the same tabs.
- **Theme** cycles light → dark → system with no menu; its outline morphs
  between sun, moon and monitor.
- **Account:** the blobatar — alive, watching the pointer — opens a menu: who is
  signed in, Profile, Settings, Notifications (soon), Log out.

## Hotkeys

`src/lib/hotkeys/registry.ts` is the dictionary: every shortcut is added there,
and menus render their keycaps from it. Rules:

- **Sequences of plain keys** — G, then S — as GitHub and Linear do. Never a
  ⌘/Ctrl combination the browser owns (⌘P prints, ⌘S saves, ⌘B shows
  bookmarks).
- Ignored while typing in a field and when a modifier is held; the next key of
  a sequence has 900 ms.
- **Escape dismisses what is in front of you**, and isn't a shortcut: no
  registry entry, no keycap. Each layer answers it for itself — bits-ui does
  for menus, lists and dialogs, and the transactions panel for its own card —
  and whoever answers marks the event, so one press closes one thing, topmost
  first. Read `defaultPrevented` rather than asking whether a layer is in the
  document: one that has closed is still there while its exit plays, and would
  swallow the next press.
- Show a shortcut wherever its action lives — beside the menu item, in the
  tooltip.

## Avatars

`Avatar` draws a [blobatar](https://blobatar.dev) through `@blobatar/svelte`,
generated in the browser and on the server — no image request leaves the app.
It's deterministic: the seed is `avatarSeed`, else the name, else the email
(v1's rule, so an identity carries over).

- **Static by default:** a single `<img>`, right for lists.
- **`animated`** comes alive on hover (inline SVG plus `blobatar/motion.css`).
- **`gaze`** is always alive — breathing, blinking — and its eyes follow the
  pointer anywhere on the page (`@blobatar/svelte/gaze`, travel 4). One per
  screen: the header's. Blobatar attaches nothing under reduced motion or
  without a fine pointer, and a still pointer schedules no frames.
- It's decorative beside a visible name or inside a labelled button; pass
  `label` only when it stands alone.

## Accessibility checklist

- Icon-only buttons have an `aria-label`; toggles say the current state and
  what comes next ("Theme: Light. Switch to Dark.").
- Focus is visible: `outline-2 outline-offset-2 outline-blue`.
- Anything revealed on hover also appears on `:focus-within`, on touch and
  while its layer is open — and stays visible when it's the only way in (no
  budget yet → the pencil shows).
- Meters expose `aria-valuetext` in words; their tooltips open on focus too.
- Colour never carries meaning alone: every tinted icon has its label beside it.

## Fields

- **A money field takes money.** Digits and separators; letters never land at
  all, whether typed or pasted (`moneyField`, which filters the element itself
  and puts the caret back — state that doesn't change would leave the rejected
  character on screen). A pasted "$1,234.00 MXN" keeps its number. Nothing is
  rounded or corrected behind the writer's back: what is out of range is said
  on saving, in words and with the limit.
- **A moment is a day and a time, side by side.** _Date_ and _Time_ share one
  row at even widths — a 12-hour clock's "p.m." needs its half — native pickers
  both. A new transaction or transfer opens on now; an edit on the row's own
  moment, in the viewer's zone. A time later than now on today is refused on
  saving, in words, as a day after today is. Reading a row, the panel says both
  as facts of their own: _Date_ ("Wednesday, September 16, 2026"), then _Time_
  ("5:23 PM").
- **An amount says what it does to the account.** Under the transactions
  panel's _Amount_, once there is an account to move — the one a transfer
  leaves, a new transaction's, or the one an old one sits on — a line gives
  its name and its balance now, and, while the amount would change it, an
  arrow and the balance after saving, counting over as it's typed. Changing a
  saved one first takes out what it already moved. Read out with the field.
- **The category is a field that is also a list** (`Combobox`): it offers every
  category already on record, most used first, each in the chip the ledger draws
  it in, and takes a name none of them has just as readily. It stays free text
  because v1's categories are — people name their own, in whichever language
  they think in — and because a first transaction has nothing to pick from.
- **Every field declares its bound** — `maxlength` from the same constant the
  endpoint checks (`MAX_CATEGORY`, `MAX_DESCRIPTION`, `MAX_MONEY_LENGTH`), so
  the field and the answer agree about what is too long.
- **`spellcheck="false"` where the content isn't prose** — amounts and
  categories. A red squiggle under a figure says it is misspelt, which is not a
  thing a figure can be.
- **The field is never the guard.** Every one of these rules is a courtesy to
  whoever is typing; the endpoint validates the same things again and is what
  actually decides.
- **What is half written survives a reload.** The transactions panel comes
  back as it was left — the row it read, its fields, a new transaction — after
  a reload or a visit to another page, and pops in as it does when opened.
  Only closing it (×, Escape, Cancel on a new one) or saving lets what was
  typed go. Storage and format: `$lib/transaction-panel`.
